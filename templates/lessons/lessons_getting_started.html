Get to know your guitar

Get comfortable before you start playing. These instructions describe right-handed playing; mirror the hands for left-handed playing.

Parts of a guitar

Headstock & tuning pegs
The headstock holds the pegs. Turn them to change string tension and pitch.
Nut, neck & frets
The nut guides strings into the neck. Press just behind a metal fret to shorten a string and change its note.
Body & sound
An acoustic guitar uses a sound hole and hollow body. An electric guitar uses pickups to send sound to an amplifier.
Bridge & saddle
These support the strings at the body end and help transfer their vibrations.

How to hold a guitar

  1. Sit upright with both feet supported and your shoulders relaxed.
  2. Rest the guitar body on your right thigh. Classical players may prefer the left thigh with a foot support.
  3. Keep the neck angled slightly upward. Rest your right forearm lightly over the body.
  4. Place your left thumb behind the neck and curve your fingers. Avoid squeezing or bending your wrist sharply.

A strap can support the guitar while standing. Adjust it so both hands can move comfortably.

How to hold a guitar pick

  1. Curl your index finger gently.
  2. Lay the pick on the side of the index finger, with the point facing the strings.
  3. Place your thumb over it, leaving a small part of the point exposed.
  4. Hold it securely, relax your wrist, and let the pick glide over the strings.

A light or medium pick is a comfortable starting point. Use small motions instead of gripping tightly.

Guitar string names

String 6 is the thickest and lowest. String 1 is the thinnest and highest.

  1. 6 · E — low E
  2. 5 · A
  3. 4 · D
  4. 3 · G
  5. 2 · B
  6. 1 · E — high E

Remember: E A D G B E, from thick to thin.

Standard guitar tuning

The open-string pitches are E2 (82.41 Hz), A2 (110.00 Hz), D3 (146.83 Hz), G3 (196.00 Hz), B3 (246.94 Hz), and E4 (329.63 Hz). The octave number distinguishes low E from high E.

  1. Open the tuner and press Start Tuner. Allow microphone access.
  2. Mute the other strings and pluck one open string. Match its note and octave to the reference.
  3. If the reading is flat, carefully increase tension. If sharp, reduce tension. Make small adjustments; avoid overtightening.
  4. Repeat for all six strings and check them once more.
